# Product Idea: ShiftPost

## Operator input

Operator runs a boutique agency booking musicians for private events. Deep
network of working musicians and venue managers. Already fields weekly requests
for last-minute fill-in players. Can reach roughly two hundred gigging musicians
by text today.

## Candidates

### C1. Last-minute fill-in marketplace

A place where a bandleader who loses a player the day of a gig posts the slot and
reaches vetted local players fast. The problem is acute and time-boxed. The wedge
is the operator's existing roster.

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Pain | 5 | A missing player hours before a paid gig is a crisis. |
| Reach | 3 | Gigging musicians in one metro, not a mass market. |
| Willingness to pay | 4 | A saved gig is worth a clear fee. |
| Founder edge | 5 | Operator already brokers these by text. |
| Feasibility | 4 | A roster plus a booking flow, no heavy build. |
| Total | 21 | |

### C2. Rehearsal-space finder

A booking tool for hourly rehearsal rooms. Broad but crowded, and the operator
has no special access to room owners.

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Pain | 2 | Annoyance, not a crisis. |
| Reach | 3 | Bands in one metro. |
| Willingness to pay | 2 | Cheap rooms, thin margin. |
| Founder edge | 1 | No existing room-owner network. |
| Feasibility | 3 | Directory plus calendar. |
| Total | 11 | |

### C3. Set-list rights helper

A tool that checks licensing for cover songs at private events. Real but niche,
and slow to prove demand.

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Pain | 3 | Occasional legal worry. |
| Reach | 2 | Only event bands that care about licensing. |
| Willingness to pay | 3 | Some pay for peace of mind. |
| Founder edge | 2 | Adjacent to the operator's network. |
| Feasibility | 2 | Needs licensing data. |
| Total | 12 | |

## Selected direction

C1, the last-minute fill-in marketplace. It wins on Pain (5) and Founder edge
(5): the crisis is real and the operator already brokers these swaps by text, so
the roster exists on day one. Target customer is the bandleader in this metro who
loses a player the day of a paid private event. The wedge is same-day access to
vetted local players the operator already knows. Why now: the operator fields
these requests weekly and turns most away for lack of a fast tool. Why this
operator: two hundred reachable musicians and standing trust with venue managers.
